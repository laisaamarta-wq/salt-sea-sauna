02 The banya

The banya.

A small wooden room above the sand, warm before you even open the door. Fifteen minutes here, and the cold that follows makes sense.

· What you notice

The glass mists over. The sea goes soft behind it.

Warmth that starts within.

The heat works slowly, first on your skin, then deeper. Your circulation wakes, your muscles soften, and the tension you carried down the boardwalk starts to let go. Your shoulders drop. Your skin turns pink.

More than a hot room, the banya is a ritual: fifteen minutes set aside to slow down, warm through and come back to your own body. People have kept it for centuries for a reason. Long-term studies link regular sauna bathing with a healthier heart, and some small studies suggest it may help the body through the cold months. We keep it simple: you leave warmer, calmer and more at home in yourself.

A ritual, not a treatment. If you have a heart condition, read “Is it safe for me?” below.

08 First time

Nervous? Good.

The questions people ask on the walk down to the water.

I've never been in cold water. Can I really do this?

Yes. You decide how deep you go, and ankles count. Your host walks in beside you and tells you what is coming before it comes. Most first-timers reach their waist; some go no further than their knees, and that is a good first time.

How long do I stay in the sea?

Thirty to ninety seconds is plenty. Get out before you start to shiver hard. Longer is not braver, just colder.

What if I panic?

The gasp is normal; it lasts a few seconds. Breathe out slowly through your mouth, then turn and walk back. The water is shallow and you can always stand.

Is it safe for me?

If you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, are pregnant, or take medicine that affects your heart, ask your doctor before you book. Don't come after drinking alcohol. Never swim out alone; at SALT you stay in the shallows.

How cold is it?

Today about 8.0 °C (example value). In January and February the Baltic sits near 0 to 2 °C; in August it reaches about 18 to 20 °C. [CHECK]

What do I bring?

A swimsuit and sandals or neoprene socks for the sand. Towels, robes and felt hats are ours. There is a warm changing room and a shower. [CHECK]

What if the sea is rough or frozen?

Sessions run all year. Your host checks the water every morning. On rough days you stay in the shallows by the rope line; when there is ice, we keep a cleared entry. [CHECK]

Can I come alone?

Yes. Most people on a first-time session come on their own. You will not be the only nervous one.
